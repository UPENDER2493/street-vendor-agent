# Business Growth & Digital Visibility Strategies for Street Vendors

> This document contains general guidance. It does NOT contain verified financial projections,
> guaranteed outcomes, or real-time market data. All suggestions are starting points — outcomes
> depend on the vendor's specific situation, location, and execution.

## Hyperlocal economy: what it means for a street vendor

A "hyperlocal economy" is the network of buyers and sellers within a small geographic area —
a neighborhood, a market, or a 1–2 km radius. For a street vendor, dominating the hyperlocal
discovery layer is often more valuable than broad city-level marketing.

Key hyperlocal channels:
- Google Maps / Google Business Profile — "near me" searches
- WhatsApp groups (colony, office, residential society)
- WhatsApp Business status
- Local social media groups (Facebook, Resident Welfare Association groups)
- Word of mouth from happy regulars

## Customer visibility: digital presence stack (recommended order)

1. **Google Business Profile** — highest ROI for local discovery; free.
2. **WhatsApp Business** — most common communication channel for Indian micro-business customers.
3. **Instagram Business** — useful for visual products (food, clothing, flowers); free.
4. **Facebook Business Page** — useful for reaching older demographics and local community groups.

None of these require a website or paid tools initially.

## Pricing considerations (general guidance — not market data)

- Price positioning: street vendor advantage is freshness + convenience + price. Reflect this.
- Bundle pricing for perceived value: bulk deals ("3 for ₹50") increase basket size.
- Simple, clear displayed prices build trust faster than negotiating every transaction.
- When input costs change, communicate transparently ("wholesale price badh gayi hai") rather than
  silently raising prices — maintains customer trust.

## Growth stages for a street vendor going digital

**Stage 1 — Discoverable:** Google Business Profile + WhatsApp Business. Customer can find you
online and contact you. (Time: 1–2 hours, Cost: ₹0)

**Stage 2 — Trustworthy:** 10+ genuine Google reviews, complete catalog with photos and prices,
consistent business hours. Customer sees social proof. (Time: 1–4 weeks, Cost: ₹0)

**Stage 3 — Engaged:** Regular WhatsApp broadcast updates (1–2/week), Google Business posts,
prompt response to messages. Customer hears from you proactively. (Time: ongoing, Cost: ₹0)

**Stage 4 — Financially formal:** UPI history, PM SVANidhi loan repaid, credit footprint building.
Access to formal credit becomes possible. (Time: 6–12 months)

**Stage 5 — Expanded:** Home delivery via WhatsApp orders, potential aggregator listing
(e.g., Dunzo, Swiggy Genie local store, ONDC), additional product lines.

## Online listing platforms worth knowing (general information)

- **ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce):** Government initiative to create an open digital
  commerce network. Local sellers can list through participating apps. Still expanding coverage.
  (ondc.org)
- **JioMart Local:** Allows local kirana and small stores to list and receive orders.
- **Amazon Local Shops:** Amazon's program for local retailers.
- **Swiggy Instamart / Blinkit:** Quick commerce platforms — primarily for established stores
  with inventory. Not typically for street vendors initially.

> IMPORTANT for the agent: The agent should NOT claim to have submitted or listed the vendor on
> any platform. These are suggestions the vendor can explore independently.

## Credit and financial growth pathway

A digital trail supports future credit access:
1. UPI transaction history (visible to bank and fintech apps)
2. PM SVANidhi repayment history (positive CIBIL signal)
3. GST registration (when turnover warrants it — not required for most street vendors)
4. Business savings account (separates personal and business transactions)

Formal credit access (Mudra loans, NBFC business loans) becomes easier once steps 1–3 are
established. The agent can explain this pathway but must NEVER guarantee a loan approval.

## What the agent should NOT promise

- NEVER guarantee specific revenue or sales growth numbers.
- NEVER guarantee ranking positions on Google.
- NEVER claim automatic registration on any platform.
- NEVER guarantee loan approval or credit access.
- NEVER claim the business will automatically become visible after any action.
- Frame all growth suggestions as "this typically helps" not "this will definitely happen."
