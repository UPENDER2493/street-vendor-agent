# Pricing Strategy & Customer Engagement for Street Vendors

> IMPORTANT: This document contains GENERAL GUIDANCE only. It does NOT contain real-time
> market prices, mandi rates, or commodity prices. The agent must NEVER present pricing guidance
> as verified market data. Always distinguish strategy from live price information.

## Pricing display best practices (general strategy — not live market prices)

- **Display prices clearly and visibly:** A small chalkboard, laminated card, or printed list
  near the stall reduces haggling time, builds trust, and signals transparency.
- **Round, simple numbers sell faster** at high-footfall stalls (₹10, ₹20, ₹50 bundles) —
  reduces mental friction vs. odd pricing like ₹47.
- **Bundle / combo pricing increases average sale value:** "3 mangoes for ₹50" vs. per-piece
  pricing often results in higher total purchase per customer.
- **Highlight "today's special" or seasonal items** with a slightly different price/colour to
  draw attention and create urgency without false scarcity claims.
- **Reprice only when needed** (e.g., when wholesale/mandi cost genuinely changes). Frequent
  unexplained price changes reduce customer trust. A short honest note like "mandi rate badha hai"
  helps retain trust during necessary price increases.
- **Unit price transparency:** For weighed goods (fruits, vegetables), displaying a per-kg rate
  alongside per-basket/bundle price helps customers calculate value quickly.

## For live commodity/mandi prices

This agent does NOT have access to live mandi prices or commodity market data.
For current mandi rates, vendors should check:
- Their local APMC mandi notice board
- State agricultural marketing board websites
- Agmarknet portal (agmarknet.gov.in) — government mandi price portal
- Local trusted suppliers and wholesalers

## Customer engagement & repeat business

- **Loyalty without an app:** A simple punch-card ("10th chai free") or memory-based recognition
  ("aapka usual order?") works extremely well for daily stalls and requires no technology.
- **WhatsApp broadcast lists:** Collect customer numbers (with consent) and send a weekly update
  about new stock, today's special, or festival offers. Keep it to 1–2 messages/week — non-spammy.
- **Respond fast to any online enquiry:** Response speed on WhatsApp or Google Business messages
  is one of the biggest conversion factors for new customers.
- **Ask for feedback casually:** "Kaisa laga?" after a sale builds relationship and surfaces
  quality issues before they become lost customers.
- **Festival/occasion offers:** Small discounts or bundles around local festivals drive footfall
  spikes and are easy to promote via WhatsApp status and Google Business posts.
- **Consistency beats promotions:** Same quality, same spot, same hours builds the strongest
  repeat-customer base over time. Promotions are a booster, not a substitute.
- **Referral encouragement:** "Apne dost ko bhi lao, dono ko special price" — word-of-mouth
  referrals are the highest-converting customer acquisition channel for street vendors.

## Simple ways to track if engagement is working

- Track roughly how many repeat vs. new faces come per week (mental note or simple tally sheet).
- Track UPI transaction count month-over-month — rising digital transactions are a proxy for
  rising footfall AND unlock PM SVANidhi digital cashback.
- Note which items sell fastest (keep more stock; promote more) vs. slowest (reduce or bundle).

## Key message for the agent

When providing pricing advice:
1. ALWAYS clarify it is general strategy, not live market data.
2. NEVER quote a specific price as current market rate unless retrieved from a verified live source.
3. ALWAYS recommend official sources (agmarknet.gov.in, local mandi) for actual commodity prices.
